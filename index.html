We built the space we needed too

We know what it is like to enter a wellness space and immediately feel the need to become smaller, quieter, or easier to understand. Some of you almost did not click because you have been disappointed before. Jess Breathe & Flow was created from that same knowing, and from the belief that healing should bring you closer to yourself.

Guided by practice, research, and lived experience

Jessica Metayer is a 500-hour advanced-trained yoga teacher, Organization Development practitioner, researcher with an MS in Organization Development from Pepperdine University's Graziadio Business School, and practitioner of meditation, breathwork, Reiki, and sound healing. Her graduate research with 15 Black executive leaders explored the hidden labor of continual proof, perception management, responsibility for others, and sustaining a sense of self within racialized organizational systems. She has since designed and led organizational culture-change engagements for teams including Workday, the International Justice Mission, and the City of Lyon's Ecological Transition Team, where she served as lead facilitator.

Rooted in community

JBAF has collaborated with the Neponset River Watershed Association, BECMA, and the City of Boston Age Strong Commission to create community-rooted wellness experiences.

Healing is not about becoming someone new.

It is about returning to the self beneath the roles, expectations, and weight you have learned to carry. High-responsibility environments can quietly require continual proof, perception management, emotional labor, and responsibility for others. Over time, that hidden work can consume capacity while becoming difficult to name.

JBAF brings together movement, breath, sound, stillness, story, and community to create the conditions for restoration and reconnection. This is not wellness that asks you to assimilate. It is a practice of remembering that rest, joy, connection, and wholeness have always belonged to you.

Healing is a homecoming.
You do not have to make the journey alone.
